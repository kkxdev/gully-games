# Pen Fight social alpha

The promise is one more school-desk Pen Fight with an old friend. Chapter 01 is
an unnamed North Indian school at lunch break in 1994. English carries the
interface; familiar Hindi/Hinglish is used sparingly and never as costume.

## Player flow

The neighbourhood opens only the School Desk. A challenger chooses the primary
Challenge a school friend action, enters a nickname, creates a private room and
shares its link. The invite contains only a six-character room code. The
private seat token remains in session storage. The friend follows the link,
enters a nickname and joins the other seat. Both ready up, play first-to-three,
then may mutually rematch (colours and opener swap), share the result, challenge
someone else, or return home.

Invalid, expired and full rooms use the same recovery surface and offer a fresh
challenge. A disconnected player has 90 seconds to return while the other sees
a countdown. Failure to reconnect abandons the room. Live rooms require a
connection; CPU practice remains offline-capable.

## Content bible

- Palette: exercise-book cream, bottle green ink, faded brick red, graphite and
  varnished brown. Avoid neon retro gradients.
- Type: sturdy serif headlines and utilitarian monospace labels, like a school
  notice and stamped stationery.
- Desk history: original scratched initials, VIII-B notation, ruled-paper
  fragments, arithmetic, pen-test strokes and restrained marginalia.
- Sound: one distant bell, slow ceiling fan, corridor murmur, knuckle/desk taps
  and distinct plastic pen impacts. Audio unlocks on deliberate interaction and
  mute is persistent.
- Voice: warm, competitive and specific without humiliation, caricatured
  accents or invented slang.
- Boundaries: no real stationery brands, school crests, copyrighted textbook
  art, identifiable uniforms, sacred imagery as decoration, or claims that one
  region represents every Indian childhood.

All visual and audio assets must be original, commissioned, self-recorded or
licensed for commercial reuse. Interview names and quotations require explicit
publication consent.

## Research checklist

Interview 8–10 adults who attended Indian schools in the 1990s; include
different cities, school systems and languages. Ask for unaided memories before
showing visuals: pen-fight rules, disputed rules, objects on desks, phrases,
sounds and what would feel false. Test the complete paper/clickable room flow
with five people, then return to at least five participants for authenticity
review. Record themes and consent status, not attributed quotations.
